# Participant Information & Informed Consent Form

**Project Title**: Human-AI Trust vs. Social Herding Behavior in Simulated Emergency Evacuations  
**Principal Investigator**: Smart-Evac Research Team  
**Institution**: Experimental Simulation Laboratory  

---

## 1. Study Purpose & Description
You are invited to participate in a research study evaluating human decision-making and visual attention during simulated emergency evacuations. The purpose of this study is to understand how individuals balance automated guidance (such as building signage or AI alerts) against observable crowd behaviors when navigating stressful spatial environments.

During this study, you will wear headphones and navigate a 3D virtual environment displayed on a desktop computer monitor using standard keyboard and mouse controls. You will complete three short evacuation trials (each lasting 15 to 45 seconds).

## 2. Potential Risks & Discomforts
- **Mild Visual Discomfort or Simulator Sickness**: Moving in a 3D first-person virtual environment may occasionally cause slight disorientation or motion sensitivity. If you experience dizziness, nausea, or visual fatigue, you may stop the experiment at any time without penalty.
- **Mild Psychological Stress**: The simulation includes audio alarms and visual representations of smoke/fire. While non-graphic and purely virtual, these stimuli are designed to simulate an evacuation context.

## 3. Voluntary Participation & Right to Withdraw
Your participation in this study is strictly voluntary. You have the right to withdraw or discontinue your participation at any moment, for any reason, without consequence.

## 4. Confidentiality & Data Anonymization
All behavioral data (navigation choices, reaction times, visual gaze coordinates) collected during the simulation are strictly anonymized using arbitrary identification codes (e.g., `P001`, `P002`). No personally identifiable information (PII) is stored in the experimental telemetry logs (`ExperimentData.csv`). Anonymized data may be used in academic publications and research presentations.
